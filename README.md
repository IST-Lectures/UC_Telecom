Comparison of the 13 teaching sheets with the September 2024 IST problem collection and published books. Reviewed 2 November 2026.

All **42 numbered problem statements** have counterparts under the **same numbers** in the 106-page *Telecomunicações — Série de Problemas*, DEEC, Instituto Superior Técnico, version 9/2024, supplied for this comparison. Problem wording, numerical data, subparts and, where distinctive, the waveform or constellation were compared. The ledger gives the printed page where each problem starts. The earlier collection includes explanatory material between some exercises, so consecutive problem numbers can be several pages apart.

The comparison concerns the **statements and their figures**. **Emmanuel Zambrini Cruzeiro wrote the worked solutions.** Francisco Sena (Francisco Alberto Sena da Silva) is acknowledged for the exercises used in the course. The 2024 collection has no visible personal byline on its cover. I found no exercise-by-exercise bibliography in it. The book findings below identify published counterparts. They do not establish who first devised an exercise or which edition the course team used. ### All 42 statements

The categories below refer only to books or teaching publications outside the IST bank. **Close** means a distinctive combination of question, parameters or figure agrees. **Partial** means an identifiable antecedent matches a subproblem or a modified setup. **Standard** denotes a documented example of a routine result or standard geometry. **Untraced** means this search did not verify a distinctive external counterpart. The IST predecessor was verified in all four categories.

### Sources and how to check them

Book problem numbers refer to the editions indicated. Where an exercise occurs in more than one book, both are listed. The publisher links identify editions. A textbook example can demonstrate prior publication without proving direct borrowing.

1. John G. Proakis and Masoud Salehi, *Fundamentals of Communication Systems*, 2nd ed., Pearson, print ISBN 9780133354850. Problems 4.2, 4.10, 5.12 and 7.6, and Fig. 8.54. [Publisher](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/pearsonplus/p/9780137848706)
2. John G. Proakis and Masoud Salehi, *Communication Systems Engineering*, 2nd ed., Prentice Hall, 2002, ISBN 9780130617934. Problem 2.39, pp. 65–66. The power-spectrum and reconstruction questions also appear in [1]. [Edition record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Communication_Systems_Engineering.html?id=ydi1QgAACAAJ)
3. Hwei P. Hsu, *Schaum’s Outline of Analog and Digital Communications*, 2nd ed., McGraw-Hill, 2003, ISBN 9780071402286. Problems 5.12, 5.18, 5.34 and 5.36. [Publisher](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/mhp/product/schaum-s-outline-analog-digital-communications.html)
4. A. Bruce Carlson and Paul B. Crilly, *Communication Systems: An Introduction to Signals and Noise in Electrical Communication*, 5th ed., McGraw-Hill, ISBN 9780073380407. Exercise 12.1–1, Example 9.1–2 and Fig. 3.1–4. [Publisher](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/communication-systems-carlson.html)
5. Apurba Das, *Digital Communication: Principles and System Modelling*, Springer, 2010. Chapter 2, “Waveform Encoding,” Example 2.2. The example uses 40 V, 256 levels and µ = 255. [Publisher and DOI](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-12743-4)
6. University of Swaziland, *EE543*, Main Examination 2015, Q2(e). The same telephone-line exercise provides dated evidence outside the books. [University exam paper](https://www.library.uneswa.ac.sz/pastpapers/quest/scie/ele/2015/ee543m2015.pdf)
7. Hwei P. Hsu, *Schaum’s Outline of Theory and Problems of Signals and Systems*, McGraw-Hill, 1995. Problem 5.54 uses an exponential with decay constant 2 and a low-pass cutoff that passes half the input energy. [University of L’Aquila course copy](https://people.disim.univaq.it/~costanzo.manes/EDU_stuff/Theory%20and%20Problems%20of%20Signals%20%26%20Systems_Hsu_Schaum95.pdf)
8. Simon Haykin and Michael Moher, *Communication Systems*, 5th ed., Wiley. Example 2.2 treats the one-sided exponential. This is also the title named on the course bibliography. A listing on a syllabus is not evidence that every exercise came from that book. [Publisher](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/communication-systems-5th-edition-p-9781118072967) · [IST bibliography](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/disciplinas/T3112/2025-2026/1-semestre/bibliografia)
9. *Basics of Wireless Communications Engineering*, University of Pisa teaching chapter, Examples 2.4 and 2.5. The examples distinguish a half-circle phase distribution from a full-circle distribution. [University course text](https://docenti.ing.unipi.it/m.luise/ComTech/Signals_Systems.pdf)
10. Upamanyu Madhow, *Fundamentals of Digital Communication*, Cambridge University Press, 2008. Chapter 3, “Demodulation,” compares the union bound for QPSK, Eq. (3.58), with the exact error probability. [Publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/fundamentals-of-digital-communication/demodulation/510C6D9C73BB804D7A42CEF6E619FCE9)
11. Rodger E. Ziemer and William H. Tranter, *Principles of Communications*, sixth edition, Problem 10.13 and Fig. 10.12. The generic Gram–Schmidt instructions resemble IST 7.1, but the pulse heights and intervals differ. This comparison is recorded so that the two exercises can be distinguished. [Book text](https://www.fccdecastro.com.br/pdf/PCSMN.pdf)
